How to read this comparison

High diversity can coexist with incoherent writing. More lexical overlap can be a refrain or a repetitive loop. Four-gram novelty does not establish creativity, and the language detector can confuse short poems or related languages.

Each configuration has 5 poems. Bootstrap intervals use 10,000 poem-level resamples and are exploratory for small samples. N-gram training size and order change together. LLM k values share themes and seeds; comparisons with n-gram poems are unpaired.

Metric definitions and rubric
Words
Length control; not a quality score.
MATTR-20 ↑
Mean type/token ratio across fixed 20-word windows; N/A below 20 words.
Distinct-2 ↑
Unique bigrams divided by all bigrams: surface diversity, not sense.
Repeat-3
Repeated trigram fraction; deliberate repetition can be effective poetry.
New 4-grams
Fraction absent from the same largest corpus; lexical novelty, not proved originality.
LID p(target)
fastText target-language probability; uncalibrated diagnostic, not verified accuracy.
Line cohesion
Adjacent-line TF-IDF cosine similarity; measures lexical overlap, not semantic coherence.
Fluency ↑
Natural diction and grammar in the target language; allow deliberate poetic fragments.
Coherence ↑
Meaningful connection and development across the poem.
Imagery ↑
Concrete, precise, evocative images.
Emotion ↑
Earned emotional resonance, rather than statements of feeling alone.
Freshness ↑
Perceived freshness and purposeful expression; not verified originality or plagiarism detection.
Craft ↑
Effective rhythm, sound and lineation; rhyme and fixed metre are optional.
Rubric mean ↑
Equal-weight descriptive mean of the six literary scores; not a universal poetry-quality index.

Rubric anchors: 1 very poor, 2 weak, 3 adequate, 4 good, 5 excellent. Free verse, ambiguity, fragments and intentional repetition are allowed; rhyme is not required. A judge should abstain when it cannot reliably assess a language.
